Wind chill and frostbiteENVIRONMENT CANADA INDEX

On a 1-in-10 night with that day's gust, the index from Jukkasjärvi onward runs −8.7 to −13.6 — Environment Canada's moderate band (−10 to −27: hypothermia and frostbite risk if outdoors for long periods without adequate protection). Nothing in thirty years of records reaches the high band (−28 and below, where exposed skin freezes in 10–30 minutes); the nearest is a summit scenario at about −24.2 at Ruka. Frostbite needs air below 0 °C — it is a summit-in-wind problem here; what arrives first everywhere is numb hands, and the wet-cold band every base sits in is where hypothermia actually happens (CDC: possible above 4 °C when wet).

Fell-hike ladder — forecast summit wind chill: ≤ −10 → shorten, add layers, hard turnaround · ≤ −18 → valley walk instead · ≤ −28 → no exposed ground. The −10 and −18 steps are our own; −28 is the cited band.

If it goes wrongSOURCED WHERE MARKED · OTHERWISE PRACTICE

  • Wet cold, not deep cold, is the threat. Warning signs (CDC): shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, slurred speech, drowsiness. Action: shelter, wet clothes off, warm the core under dry layers, warm non-alcoholic drink only if fully conscious, handle gently, medical help as soon as possible. With signal, 112; without, the satellite messenger SOS at the same moment — rescue in Lapland is hours away. The messenger needs a clear view of the sky: move to a clearing if a message will not send.
  • Black ice. Wet afternoon, clearing sky, still cold night → glazed road at first light that looks merely damp. Most frequent set-up at Ruka, Saariselkä and Koli. The trail days start at first light and the 24 Oct ferry drive starts before it. Speed and following distance are the controls (Liikenneturva).
  • Stuck overnight (NWS): stay in the vehicle; run the engine about 10 minutes each hour, window cracked, exhaust cleared of snow; dome light on at night, bright cloth on the door, bonnet up when the snow stops. Keep above half a tank on remote sections so that routine lasts two nights; a battery CO alarm per vehicle. Convoy: the second vehicle stops short of a soft patch and assesses before following — two vehicles are the recovery plan only until both are stuck.
  • Animals (Liikenneturva): elk move an hour either side of dusk and dawn, September–November — the same hours as the ice and, from mid-October, the same hours you drive. Reindeer everywhere in Lapland, in herds. Slow down on unlit roads, never swerve, 112 after any collision.
  • Trail-day snow: none of the three stations has recorded snow on the ground in the window in its observed years — Ullånger 15, Granö 21, Harads 16 (short series; read as uncommon, not never) — a 06:00 valley reading that says nothing about daytime showers or higher forest roads. Frost on 35–43 % of nights and a wet day on 28–34 % of days. The likely trail hazard is mud, ruts and standing water after rain, then frozen puddles and glazed shade at dawn.
